# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

rslvd.net is a small project run by volunteers and paid for by donations. Everyone should be able to
take part — reporting a bug, asking a question, opening a pull request, or just using the service —
without being harassed or made to feel unwelcome, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity
and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be direct, not unkind.** Review the code, not the person. "This query is not parameterised, so it
  breaks on a quote in the email" is useful; "this is amateur hour" is not.
- **Assume good faith and ask before concluding.** Most "obviously wrong" code in this repo is a
  workaround for something that bit us in production.
- **Respect the project's constraints.** rslvd.net is deliberately small: no build step, few
  dependencies, one server. A feature request does not entitle you to a rewrite.
- **Be careful with other people's data.** Do not post someone's IP, hostname, email, tunnel token or
  traffic captures. Use your own test accounts and hosts.
- **Accept "no" gracefully.** Maintainers may decline a contribution, close an issue, or ask you to
  take a different approach. That is not a personal attack.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted attention of any kind.
- Insults, personal or political attacks, and dismissive or demeaning comments.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Discriminatory jokes or comments, including about the protected characteristics listed above.
- Publishing someone's private information (doxxing) without explicit permission.
- Threatening to expose a vulnerability, or to publish someone's data, to force a response.
- Sustained disruption: spamming issues or PRs, reopening closed threads to relitigate, or derailing
  technical discussion.
- Encouraging or helping others do any of the above.

This applies in every project space — the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code comments, and direct messages about the project — and it applies to maintainers too.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour to **conduct@rslvd.net**. Include what happened, where, when, and links or
screenshots if you have them. Reports are read by the maintainer(s); they are handled privately, and
your identity is not shared with the person you are reporting unless you say you are comfortable with
it.

If the report concerns the maintainer, say so — it will be handled by an outside party the project
designates, or, if no such party exists, disclosed to the community at the maintainer's discretion with
your privacy preserved.

We will acknowledge a report within 5 business days and tell you what we intend to do.

## Enforcement

Maintainers take the following ladder as guidance, not a straitjacket; severity and history decide.

1. **Correction.** A private note explaining what was inappropriate and why, and asking for a change in
   behaviour. No further action if it lands.
2. **Warning.** A formal warning with consequences for repeating the behaviour, including a period of
   no interaction with the people involved.
3. **Temporary ban.** A timeout from all project spaces for a stated period (typically 30 days). No
   interacting with the people involved, publicly or privately, during that time.
4. **Permanent ban.** Removal from all project spaces for repeated or severe violations, including
   harassment, threats, or doxxing.

Maintainers may also edit or delete comments, commits, code, issues and PRs that violate this policy,
and will explain the reason when they do. Anyone asked to stop unacceptable behaviour is expected to
comply immediately.

Appeals go to **conduct@rslvd.net**; a decision will be reviewed by someone who did not make it.

## Scope

This policy covers project spaces. It also covers behaviour outside them when it directly affects the
safety of project participants — for example, harassment of a contributor on social media because of
their work here.

It does **not** cover what users host behind their own tunnels. Content on a user's tunnel is between
that user and the law; the [Terms of Service](https://rslvd.net/terms) govern acceptable use of the
service, and abuse should go to **abuse@rslvd.net**, not to this policy.
